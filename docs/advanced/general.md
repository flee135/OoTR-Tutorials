# General Advice

## Analyze seeds

If you're playing seeds solo, and anything wasn't clear, like why certain locations were path and what those path items were, it's beneficial to look over the spoiler log and figure out why. Once you've figured it out, also ask yourself if it would have been feasible to figure it out in the middle of the seed, and if it would have been helpful in any way. Sometimes it wouldn't have actually helped in the seed, but that's fine. Figuring out these kind of things in a non-race environment help make it easier to solve when you're actually racing.

If you're playing race seeds, then you can also compare routes with the other players in the race and see if the overall decisions were the same. You can also get a sense of how strong your execution by skimming VODs and seeing if another top player had an identical opener as you. If you find the point where your routes diverged, then the difference in your times at that point is a rough indicator of how much time you're losing or gaining due to execution, plus maybe some small micro-routing.

The same goes for full clears of dungeons. You can look at how long a particular clear took, and see if that holds up to another player with the same loadout. If there are big differences, you can watch more carefully, or play the VODs side by side to see what causes them to drift apart.

## Watch Top Players

It's easy to just keep grinding out seeds, which helps build consistency. However, you don't know what you don't know, and it is very easy to build consistency in bad habits without knowing it. If you watch others, you'll learn about tricks or optimizations that you had never even considered. All top players have copied movement or strats from other top players, and I can pinpoint so many things that I do back to the specific players that I copied from.

If you're looking to improve, you need to be **actively** watching too, not just having a stream open on a separate monitor while multitasking. This means:

- Thinking about what you would do in certain scenarios and comparing with what the player is doing. If there's disagreement, think about why that might be, pros and cons of your route vs theirs. You may conclude that your idea was better, and that's fine. But try to be open to new ideas that you may not have considered before.

- Pay attention to execution, and see if they do anything differently than you would have. This can be as big as how they route a dungeon, or as small as how many rolls they do to get around in a specific part of a map.

- **Take notes.** If you see them do a trick you've never seen, or a dungeon route you've never taken, write it down. Next time you boot up the game, go to the Practice ROM first and try those things out. Time it. See if it's worth incorporating. You may think you can remember to do this, but what if they do another thing you haven't seen, or a third thing, or a tenth thing? You're going to forget some of them.

If you're watching a streamer and it looks like they're doing a practice seed or a casual race, don't be afraid to ask questions. Most people are more than happy to answer questions or explain their thought process.

The next one is a personal opinion that you might disagree with, but for learning, I much prefer watching a single player's perspective vs a restream for a race with multiple perspectives. It's certainly possible to learn from these, but it's also easy to get swayed by having more information than all of the runners individually, or thinking one decision is better than the another based on the race result.

## There is luck involved in rando

Sometimes it doesn't matter how well you played, you will sometimes lose to simply getting unlucky. An important skill in rando is to recognize when this happens, and not overcompensate for it. Take a look at the winning routes, compare it with yours, and be honest with yourself about which was better. If yours was truly better, then shrug it off and better luck next time.

**This goes in the other direction as well.** Don't get a big head if you win a race. Think about all the decisions you made in the seed, and if you really made the best plays throughout. You'll have plenty of seeds where you made a bad play that just happened to have what you needed. Acknowledge it, laugh about it, but try to avoid it next time.

Understand and accept that luck is a big part of the game. This helps you pick out parts of your game that you can actually improve, and avoid adjusting things that were actually fine to begin with. This is also a healthy attitude in general and can help with getting burnt out.

## Minimize Gambles / Metagaming

I have seen people opt to take gambles and make risky plays when they play an opponent that they believe to be better than themselves. Some people might also metagame and think along the lines of "My opponent has probably done X, therefore...". This does sometimes have its place, but doing this too much can stifle improvement.

Another common thing I see is for people to take more gambles as perceived mistakes start to pile up, such as "If this location has the item I need, I've already lost, so I'm going to play as if the item isn't there". But the truth is, you can't ever know what your opponent is doing, and you never know if they've made the same mistake or not.

The default should be to making decisions that you believe to be optimal at any given point. The goal is always to try to get the lowest possible time. So at any point in the seed, regardless of what you've done earlier, you should be making decisions that, on average, minimize your finish time.
