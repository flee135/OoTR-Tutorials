# Routing

Routing in randomizer is a loaded term. It can refer to what I'm going to call micro-routing, which is when you are in a region with a particular loadout, and need to follow a route to efficiently clear out the region. This has huge overlap with execution, because once you know these routes, you almost always do them the same way every single time. [Dungeon routing](./execution.md/#dungeons), covered in the execution tutorials, falls into this category.

Routing can also refer to macro-routing, which is making decisions on what to do next based on your loadout. Usually when people talk about routing, this is what they refer to, and is what will be covered here.

>**Note:** This section is dense with a lot of stuff to learn, and it's probably not a good idea to learn everything here before jumping into your next seed. I recommend learning a little bit at a time, mixing it in with reading [Execution](./execution.md) as well, and revisiting both pages between seeds to learn more or reinforce what you've already learned.

There are two very well known videos about routing from atz, who was considered the best player by far in the early days of rando. While these videos are now quite old and the seeds played back are very different from today's seeds, they are still very good videos that cover the fundamentals of routing.

If you'd like to watch these, you can visit this page on [atz's routing tutorials](./atz-routing.md), which has the videos embedded, alongside my own notes that you can follow with the video to help contextualize some of his tips for modern rando. For the rest of the tutorial, we first go over broad concepts to help understand what makes a good play. These help inform decisions on where to go and what to do. From there, we talk about what to focus on in different phases of the game with those concepts in mind.

## Progression

The most important thing to look out for is progression. Progression is defined as anything that you *need* to do in order to beat the seed. For example, the 6 medallion dungeons are all progression dungeons that need to be completed at some point. Path hints are another form of progression since path locations contain items that are logically required to beat medallion dungeons, though it is worth noting that these items are sometimes not *strictly* required and might be possible to skip.

When you have the means to do progression, it is often correct to get around to it sooner rather than later. For example, if Forest Temple is a medallion dungeon, then when you find that you have bow, hookshot, and strength, which is everything required to full clear Forest Temple, that is very likely one of the next locations that you should visit. While other locations might be closer or be higher density, you need to clear Forest at some point in the seed. Doing it earlier means that if Forest contains any progression items, you get those items earlier. This can then in turn open up more progression locations.

It's worth emphasizing again that progression is only what you *need* to do. Fire Temple may be a progression dungeon, but if you find boss key on the very first check, everything else in Fire is no longer considered progression. This is also true in other non-linear dungeons, such as Water or Spirit. Of course, that doesn't necessarily mean you just drop doing the rest of the dungeons when you find boss key. You need to make a judgement call on whether or not to continue deeper into the dungeon based on other factors such as efficiency, other progression you have available in the seed, and how easy it is to return back to where you are if you do need to backtrack.

## Density

When you're out of progression, you'll want to focus on density. This means prioritizing going to regions that have a lot of checks available, and preferably in logic, or you know for sure will be in logic at some point. These are statistically the most likely regions to have items that you need in order to progress.

## Efficiency

Efficiency involves doing all the checks in the game in a way that minimizes backtracking. When given the option between two otherwise equal locations to go to, pick the one that you can clear more of, or the one that is easier to return to in the future in case you do need to backtrack.

If Bottom of the Well starts becoming the best available play, but you're still missing ZL, maybe you go collect a couple more easy songs prior to going. You won't always find it, though, and you'll have to decide in a lot of seeds if you want to visit locations before being able to full clear. It's always possible Bottom of the Well has a crucial item that eventually leads you to ZL, in which case putting it off is actually detrimental.

While clearing a location, you may find an item that opens up a better location to be doing. When in doubt, I recommend finishing up the region you're currently in before leaving. Of course, there are exceptions, and you can leave early if you think you won't need to come back later in the seed.

## Importance of Songs

Songs are the most important items in the game. In most settings, songs are only shuffled amongst themselves, meaning that locations that contain songs will always be very strong locations despite their check density. Lon Lon Ranch is a good example of this, where there are only three checks, but almost every runner will prioritize it early for the guaranteed song.

It is worth knowing why each song is good, especially to have early.

---

**ZL** - It's required in nearly all seeds. It prevents potential backtracking to one-off fairy fountains, and it also logically locks two more songs. 

**Epona's** - Not too useful early game. Can help traverse the overworld and reach GTG. Utility increases if you also find bow, hook, bombs early to do most of GTG, and prevents backtracking for HBA.

**Saria's** - Prevents backtracking to LW as child.

**Sun's** - Don't need to worry about time of day.

**SoT** - Not so useful early game.

**Storms** - Opens up BotW, which is one of the densest locations in the game.

---

The following are all warp songs, so they all have the shared utility of speeding up overworld movement, often to otherwise pretty remote locations.

**Minuet** - Gives easy access to two song checks. Can be logically required for Forest access.

**Bolero** - Warps to another song check.

**Serenade** - Warp to one of the most remote locations in the game. As child, it can also serve as a quick way back to market/ToT if you don't have prelude.

**Requiem** - Gives another song check. Very strong if Spirit is progression. If it is, getting access to child spirit is very strong since just explosives and a way to lower the bridge is all you need to clear it.

**Nocturne** - Required if you need to complete Shadow.

**Prelude** - Makes age changes practically free. Without it, you typically have to commit to clearing out more of your current age before switching.

Notice that several warp songs actually lead straight to more songs. This means in the early game, some songs that you find are actually effectively be two songs in one.

## Early Game

You have two main goals in the early game:

- Route in as many efficient sphere 0 checks as you can.
- Gather songs.

Since a lot of settings nowadays use random starting age and/or random spawns, it's not really viable to think of strict openers to follow for every seed. Instead, it's more useful to think of "what locations should I clear out before I'm truly in the mid game."

As we've established, songs are very strong early on, and this heavily informs which locations we should be hitting at the start.

---

As Child:

- **Lon Lon Ranch** - Has a song, and the entire region is full clearable in sphere 0.

- **KF/LW/SFM** - There is a song in SFM, and lots of sphere 0 checks.

As Adult:

- **Kak/Graveyard** - Has a song, and lots of sphere 0 checks.

---

Depending on starting age and spawns, how you approach these will vary.

Example 1: Let's assume child start and vanilla spawns. You would start by clearing KF/LW/SFM since you're already there, then go to Lon Lon, then go change age and do adult Kak.

Example 2: Let's assume adult start and vanilla spawns. You can go straight to adult Kak, and when you're done clearing it, savequit so that you spawn back in Temple of Time. Then you go child, do market into Lon Lon, then savequit from the back of Lon Lon to put yourself in KF where you can do KF/LW/SFM.

---

One thing I will go into a little more detail is what to do after you've done Lon Lon and you find yourself in the very back. If your savequit is not a useful spawn, and you don't have any good warp songs to get out, then you have a some options.

I recommend walking out and going to valley (2 checks, 1 hint), lake (1 check, peek underwater, 1-3 hints depending on if you do back of lake hints, set up scarecrow song), field open grotto (1 check, 1 hint), into Kokiri Forest. If you have explosives you can get two more checks plus a hint in south field. This results in a more thorough child section, gives you a lot of hints to guide you through the mid game, and prevents potential backtracking in the late game.

As you improve, you can consider just going straight to Forest from Lon Lon instead. This defers your decision of if you want to clear valley/lake until you're closer to your mid-game.

---

It's worth noting that early game is not limited to these locations; you can choose to do more depending on where you are and what you've found. For example, if you start with adult Kak and find bombs, and DC is a progression dungeon, it's usually worth going up trail and at least clearing DC. You have to do it eventually anyway, and you may find more items that can lead to a more thorough child section. If you then find hook or hovers, you may consider going to DMC to get the bolero song, which might be an additional warp song which speeds up your child section.

When in doubt, though, it's not bad to stick to only the early game locations.

## Mid Game

After you've cleared your early game locations, you typically have a variety of hints and items at your disposal, and it's up to you to figure out where to go from there.

### Progression

This is the point in the game where you want to heavily prioritize progression. If you have required dungeons that you are able to full clear, it's often a good idea to make your way there, taking a route with a lot of other checks along the way, and clearing those dungeons. 

### Paths Hints

If you have any hints towards any dungeons that you are still missing items for, it can often be a good idea to prioritize going to those locations. These are not as strong as progression locations because the hints may only lead to items that are only logically required to beat the seed, not hard required. In the absence of any other information though, chasing path hints is often a good play.

There are some exceptions where you might want to put off path locations. This usually stems from a dense location being hinted path that you cannot clear a lot of. For example, you have Epona's early, and GTG is hinted path, but you don't have any of bow, hook, or bombs. You're only guaranteed to be able to do one check in the dungeon out of the many, and you're almost certainly better off doing other dense locations over rushing this path location.

### Density

If you have no indication of where you should be going next, pick regions that are dense to increase your odds of fishing out an item that opens up other parts of the world. When weighing check density between different regions, try to take hint stones into consideration as well, especially if you are still missing path hints. These may very well be what help push you towards the right locations to help unblock you.

If all else is equal, prefer to play thoroughly, and pick locations that you can full clear over ones that you cannot to minimize backtracking that you may have to do later.

## Late Game

You enter late game when you've completed most of your progression dungeons and you're really just looking for one or two more key items that will help you finish the seed. All the principles from the mid game still hold, and you can continue to keep playing with these in mind.

While you're looking for the last few items, how you play does not drastically change from the mid game. However, this is the point in the run where having a more efficient clear in the early/mid game minimizes the amount of backtracking you have to do to one-off locations in the late game, where each one of these checks is drastically more expensive than they would have been if routed in earlier.

### Go Mode

Once you find that one item you need, you've hit go mode, and you should drop everything you're doing and only do progression to make it to the end of the game.

You may sometimes hit a point where you're *potentially* in go mode. An example is if you're halfway through clearing Bottom of the Well and find iron boots to beat Water Temple, your final progression dungeon, but you don't have Song of Time which was hinted vanilla on the Ocarina of Time check, locked behind getting all three stones. There is a chance your Water BK is locked behind Song of Time, so it's up to you if you clear the rest of Bottom of the Well in case it still has something needed to clear your stone dungeons, or if you bail immediately and go straight Water.

In this case it's pretty clear to most that you should just bail and play optimistically; after all, the chance of the BK being in the one chest you can't reach is pretty low. If you have FW, you can always play it safe and drop it wherever you are so you can easily pick up where you left off if you discover that you're not in go mode.

Other cases might not be so clear cut. In these cases, it's up to you to decide if you keep playing for efficiency or not. You'll want to consider things like "how hard is it to get back to where I am now" and "what are the chances I'm in go mode?"

### Glow Mode

If you have all the items to reach Ganondorf, but not the light arrows, you are in what is known as glow mode. The same rules apply as when you're potentially in go mode. If you're in a location that's particularly difficult to get back to, consider clearing it first. Otherwise, rush straight to Ganon for the light arrow hint and narrow down where your lights are. If it's in a location you know you can full clear, then you've confirmed you're in go mode, and if not, you're back to potentially needing to play efficiently again until you find the items you need.
